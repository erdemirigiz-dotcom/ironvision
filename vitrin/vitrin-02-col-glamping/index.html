A night at HALLEY

The desert keeps time by starlight

No screens, no schedule you didn't choose. Just a slow arc from last light to first — the way people read the sky before there were cities to forget it.

  1. 18:30

    Blue hour arrival

    Tea by the fire pit as the plateau cools and the first planets appear. Your host walks you to your tent while there is still light on the dunes.

  2. 21:00

    Telescope stargazing

    A guided hour at the Dobsonian: rings of Saturn, the craters along the moon's terminator, and whichever deep-sky objects are highest that night.

  3. 23:30

    The silence

    The guides step back. You lie on a heated daybed under a sky rated Bortle 2 — dark enough that the Milky Way casts a faint shadow.

  4. 05:40

    Desert breakfast at dawn

    Cardamom coffee and warm bread as the horizon turns from violet to amber and the stars quietly hand the sky back to the sun.

Before you book

Honest answers

What happens if it's cloudy?

Skies here are clear on roughly 270 nights a year, but weather is weather. If cloud cover blocks the stargazing session entirely, we run an indoor astronomy talk and give you a voucher for a free return telescope night within 12 months. Your stay itself is never charged twice.

Is the telescope session included?

Yes. Every booking includes the guided hour at the telescope with a resident astronomy guide. No extra fee, no upsell. Serious astrophotographers can request the observation deck and a mount for their own gear when booking.

Can we bring children?

The Caravan tent is built for families and sleeps up to four. Children are welcome at the early telescope session; the later silent hours are quieter and best suited to older kids and adults. There is no minimum age.

What's your cancellation policy?

Free cancellation up to 7 days before arrival for a full refund. Within 7 days we hold your deposit as credit toward any future date for a year. We'd rather you came under a sky you're excited about than one you're stuck with.

When is the best time of year to come?

Every season shows a different sky. Winter brings Orion and crisp, steady air; summer brings the bright core of the Milky Way and Scorpius low on the horizon. Pick a date near a new moon for the darkest possible sky — the map on this page shows you the moon phase for any night you choose.

How do we get there?

The camp is about a three-hour drive from the nearest regional airport, the last stretch on graded desert track. We offer a private 4×4 transfer from the airport or the nearest town, arranged after you book. Self-drive guests receive detailed offline directions.
